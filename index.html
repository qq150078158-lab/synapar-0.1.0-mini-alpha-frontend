The current model was trained exclusively on a subset of historical A-stocks and BTC data. Consequently, its performance in non-A-stocks markets is expected to be comparatively weaker, although it still retains a degree of generalization capability. 3. Due to the extreme scarcity of high-frequency (i.e., sub-daily) data within the training set, the current version exhibits poor performance on high-frequency data. 4. For identical inputs, the model's generated outputs are precisely reproducible. 5. A longer maximum context length generally correlates with higher end-point returns. 6. The model's output comprises three key elements: direction, quantity, and leverage. 6.1 Direction: Includes three distinct instructions — Long, Short, and Hold — along with their respective confidence scores. 6.2 Quantity: A floating-point value within the [-1, 1] range. A positive value signifies the proportion of available capital to be used for opening or increasing a position. A negative value signifies the proportion of current holdings to be liquidated or reduced. 6.3 Leverage: A floating-point value within the [0, 1] range, indicating the proportion of the permissible leverage (e.g., within a 1x to 20x range) to apply to the trade. This value is not applicable when closing or reducing a position. 7. Synapar is a Large Decision Model (LDM). It aims to establish a general-purpose infrastructure for generating a theoretically near-optimal trading instruction for the latest time step, based on a context of a given length — specifically, financial time-series data of a particular frequency. Admittedly, trading rules differ among various financial markets and products, and investors operate under diverse self-imposed constraints. Therefore, the application of LDM-generated trading instructions necessitates flexible adaptation in accordance with the specific context. 8. Future iterations of the model are planned to cover major global financial markets and products, including equities, futures, forex, and cryptocurrencies. The vision is for Synapar to become a general-purpose infrastructure for AI-generated trading instructions, accessible via API services.
Trading Rules
For ease of understanding and comparison, simulated trading followed these simplified rules: 1. T+0 bidirectional trading is adopted. 2. No leverage is used. 3. Opening and adding to positions are executed with 50% of the current available funds; reducing positions is done with 50% of the current holdings; closing positions are executed in their entirety. 4. When the model issues a trading signal opposite to the current holding, it is treated as a signal to close the position and immediately open a reverse position. 5. The simulated execution price and asset valuation price are both simplified to use the current K-line's closing price. 6. The fee rates are set to 0.5% for opening positions and 0.8% for closing them. 7. To ensure sufficient historical context, no trades are made in the first 128 steps of each sequence.
About Metrics
1. End-point Returns: Measured based on the number of steps (i.e., K-lines) rather than time span. The calculation length is the maximum context length minus 128. Furthermore, to ensure minimal feature construction, no trades are executed within the initial 128 steps. 2. Sharpe Ratio: The benchmark risk-free rate is set at 3%. 3. Max Drawdown: For testing purposes, the simulated trading does not implement stop-loss risk control measures triggered by a maximum drawdown threshold. In real-world scenarios, this threshold should be set appropriately based on individual risk tolerance.

Synapar is a Large Decision Model (LDM) utilizing an innovative Hierarchical Temporal-Spatial (HTS) architecture for financial time-series trading decisions. The LDM, a concept introduced herein, aims to establish a general-purpose infrastructure for generating a theoretically near-optimal trading instruction for the latest time step, based on a context of a given length — specifically, financial time-series data of a particular frequency. Admittedly, trading rules differ among various financial markets and products, and investors operate under diverse self-imposed constraints.
